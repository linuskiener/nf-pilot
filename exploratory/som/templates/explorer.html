Transcript Concept Atlas

Two self-organizing maps of the transcripts. On the word map every square holds lemmas that are used in the same stretches of talk, so neighbouring squares hold related concepts. On the segment map every square holds ~200-word transcript segments about the same thing, and the heatmap layers show when, where and in which format a topic is talked about.

Thick lines separate the k-means regions of the map. The grey tag nXeY means node X holding Y words, as in the book. Click a square to list all of its words.
Hover a square for its prototype's strongest terms, click it to read the three segments closest to it. Hatched squares hold fewer than three segments.

How the maps were made

  • Unit of observation. The word map follows the book: a word is an observation and is described by its contexts. The contexts are the ~200-word segments from the existing segmentation step, so a long livestream counts as many contexts and a Short as one. The segment map treats each segment as an observation, as in the Reuters paper.
  • Cleaning. Caption markers ([Musik], [Applaus], broken SRT time stamps) are removed. spaCy (de_core_news_sm) reduces every word to its lemma and keeps nouns, proper nouns, adjectives and verbs; stop words and spoken fillers are dropped. Punctuation is never used, so captions with and without punctuation are treated the same.
  • Vocabulary. Lemmas found in at least 20 segments of at least 8 different videos. That keeps transcription errors of single streams off the map but keeps frequent mis-hearings such as "März" for Merz, which then sit right next to the correct spelling.
  • Vectors. Word map: positive PMI of words sharing a segment, compressed to 200 dimensions with SVD. Segment map: TF-IDF of the segment, compressed the same way. Both are length-normalised, so distance on the map means cosine distance.
  • Training. torchsom, rectangular grid, PCA initialisation, Gaussian neighbourhood shrinking from a quarter of the map to one cell. Regions are k-means clusters of the trained prototypes.
  • Heatmaps. U-matrix (distance of a square to its neighbours: high = a border between topics), hit map (how many words or segments landed there), component planes (weight of one term in each prototype), and "faces": how much more or less a month or a format uses a square than the corpus as a whole, in log2 units, shrunk towards zero where there is little data.

The maps are exploratory. Squares near the centre of the word map collect general verbs and adjectives that occur everywhere; the topical vocabulary sits towards the edges and corners.
